\begin{abstract}
We record and machine-check two polynomial identities that underlie the de Broglie--Bohm guidance law and the Bohm quantum-potential prefactor, in a form cleared of all denominators. Writing $\psi=(Rc,Rs)$ and the product-rule derivative $d\psi=(dR\,c-Rs\,dS,\ dR\,s+Rc\,dS)$, we prove that $\operatorname{Im}(d\psi\cdot\overline{\psi})=dS\,|\psi|^2$ holds as an identity in the integers $\mathbb{Z}$ for arbitrary values of $R,dR,c,s,dS$, and that $(-\Delta R)(2R)=2\,((-\Delta R)R)$. The proofs are carried out in Lean~4 using the core library only (no Mathlib), contain no \texttt{sorry}, and depend on no axioms beyond Lean's standard ones. The paper states precisely what is formalized and what is not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In the Madelung (polar) representation $\psi=Re^{iS}$ of a wave function, the de Broglie--Bohm velocity field is $\nabla S=\operatorname{Im}(\nabla\psi/\psi)$ wherever $\psi\neq0$, and the quantum potential is $Q=-\tfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\,\Delta R/R$. Both statements are, at bottom, algebraic identities once the derivatives are expressed through the product rule. This note isolates those identities in their \emph{cleared} (division-free) form and verifies them in the Lean~4 proof assistant.

The formal development is deliberately elementary: all quantities are integers and the identities are polynomial. The contribution is a short, fully checked statement of exactly which algebra the guidance law rests on. Analytic content (differentiability, the Schr\"odinger equation, winding numbers, the harmonic-oscillator spectrum, split-step integrators) is \emph{not} formalized here.

\section{Setting}
Fix integers $R,dR,c,s,dS$. Here $c,s$ play the role of $\cos S,\sin S$ (no relation $c^2+s^2=1$ is assumed), $dR$ and $dS$ the derivatives of amplitude and phase along one direction. Put
\[
\psi=(Rc,\ Rs),\qquad d\psi=(dR\,c-Rs\,dS,\ \ dR\,s+Rc\,dS).
\]
The second formula is the product rule for $R\cdot(c,s)$ with $dc=-s\,dS$, $ds=c\,dS$; in the formalization it is a \emph{definition of the input}, i.e.\ a hypothesis on the shape of $d\psi$, not something derived.

For $z=(a,b)$, $w=(a',b')$ regarded as complex numbers $a+ib$, $a'+ib'$, one has $\operatorname{Im}(z\overline{w})=ba'-ab'$ and $|w|^2=a'^2+b'^2$.

\section{Results}
\begin{theorem}[Cleared guidance law; \lean{Bohm.guidance_cleared}]\label{thm:guidance}
For all $R,dR,c,s,dS\in\mathbb{Z}$,
\[
(dR\,s+Rc\,dS)(Rc)-(dR\,c-Rs\,dS)(Rs)\;=\;dS\,\bigl((Rc)(Rc)+(Rs)(Rs)\bigr).
\]
That is, $\operatorname{Im}(d\psi\cdot\overline{\psi})=dS\,|\psi|^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Over a field, dividing by $|\psi|^2\neq0$ gives $\operatorname{Im}(d\psi/\psi)=dS$, the guidance law. The division step is \emph{not} part of the formalization; only the cleared identity of Theorem~\ref{thm:guidance} is proved.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Cleared quantum-potential prefactor; \lean{Bohm.quantumPotential_cleared}]\label{thm:qp}
For all $\Delta R,R\in\mathbb{Z}$, $(-\Delta R)(2R)=2\,\bigl((-\Delta R)R\bigr)$.
\end{theorem}
This is the statement that $-\tfrac12\,\Delta R/R$ and $-\Delta R/(2R)$ agree after clearing the common denominator $2R$.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:guidance} uses three auxiliary lemmas, all proved for arbitrary integers:
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:aux}
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\lean{Bohm.add_neg_self}) $a+(-a)=0$.
\item (\lean{Bohm.cross}) $(dR\,s)(Rc)=(dR\,c)(Rs)$: the two cross terms of the product rule coincide.
\item (\lean{Bohm.phaseTerm}) $(Rc\,dS)(Rc)=dS\,\bigl((Rc)(Rc)\bigr)$: a phase term factors out $dS$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:guidance}]
Expand the left side. The two cross terms $(dR\,s)(Rc)$ and $-(dR\,c)(Rs)$ cancel by Lemma~\ref{lem:aux}(2),(1). The remaining terms $(Rc\,dS)(Rc)+(Rs\,dS)(Rs)$ equal $dS(Rc)(Rc)+dS(Rs)(Rs)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:aux}(3), and distributivity gives the right side. The Lean proof follows exactly this calculation using rewriting with core \texttt{Int} lemmas.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope}
The formalization proves the five integer identities above and nothing more. In particular it does not formalize: the division $\operatorname{Im}(d\psi/\psi)=dS$; any real or complex analysis; the Schr\"odinger or continuity equations; quantization of circulation or winding; the oscillator eigenvalue; or any numerical scheme.
